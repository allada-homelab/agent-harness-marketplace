INC-2291 Checkout Outage

Post-incident review of the 51-minute checkout outage on 22 Sep 2026, caused by database connection exhaustion after a pool-size change.

Maya Okafor, payments on-call · reviewed with Platform SRE Updated

Summary

From 14:07 to 14:58 UTC, 6.8% of checkout attempts failed and the rest slowed to a 9.2 s p95. Release 3.41.0 of checkout-api raised each pod's connection pool from 20 to 60; when the lunch peak scaled the service to 14 pods, they asked the primary database for more connections than it allows. Rolling back the release restored service. We are moving pool sizing to a fleet-wide budget and putting PgBouncer in front of the primary.

Contents

    Impact

    For 51 minutes, customers on web and in the iOS and Android apps saw checkout spin and then fail with “We couldn’t place your order.” Of 60,600 checkout attempts in the window, 4,120 failed. Baskets were kept, and support counted 312 contacts about duplicate charges, all of which were authorisations that expired without capture.

    • Checkout p95 latency rose from 380 ms to 9.2 s at the worst minute, 14:31.
    • Order history and saved cards were slow but available; browsing and search were unaffected.
    • No data was lost. Every failed attempt rolled back before payment capture.

    Root cause

    The primary database accepts 800 connections, with 30 reserved for superusers and replication.1 Other services hold up to 170 at peak, which leaves checkout-api 600. Release 3.41.0 raised HikariCP’s maximumPoolSize from 20 to 60 to absorb short bursts in a load test that ran on a single pod.2 At 10 pods the fleet could open 600 connections, exactly its share. At 14 pods it could open 840.

    The pods that started at 14:07 could not get connections, failed readiness and were restarted, and each restart opened a fresh pool while the old connections were still closing. The retry policy on the checkout client then multiplied the load on the pods that were still healthy.3

    The change passed review because pool size is set per pod, while the limit that matters is the total across the fleet at its maximum scale. Nothing in our config or CI connects the two numbers.

    Decision

    Recommendation

    Treat database connections as a fleet-wide budget. Each service declares its share of the primary’s 770 usable connections, and CI fails any change where pool size times the autoscaler’s maximum replicas exceeds that share.

    We considered raising max_connections instead. Each Postgres connection is a process with its own memory, and doubling the limit would trade this failure for memory pressure on the primary.4 A pooler in transaction mode lets many client connections share a small number of server connections, which addresses the cause.

    Open risk

    Until PgBouncer is in place, checkout-api’s share of 600 connections allows 30 pods at pool size 20, and a restart briefly holds two pools. The autoscaler is capped at 24 pods to keep that headroom.
